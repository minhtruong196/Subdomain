Analytical-code loaded torque check:
The first loaded torque sweep above is FEMM base vs FEMM equivalent only. To test code torque, use a Lorentz/winding-function estimate:
slot force = axial slot current * stack length * no-load Br at the slot, then torque = radius * force.
This ignores armature reaction, so it is a practical first check rather than a full current-field subdomain model.

Analytical geometry sensitivity update:
paper5_radial_shift_validation.py now has an optional geometry-only PM-to-airgap permeance scaling:
gain = (gap_ref / gap) ** p
where gap = field_evaluation_radius - PM_outer_radius. This is not fitted from the radial-shift sweep. With p=1 it is the first-order inverse-gap response of a simple magnetic circuit. The default is p=0 to preserve old results.

Paper bridge permeability / mu note:
- The paper title says bridge nonlinearity is considered through MEC/equivalent magnetic-bridge permeability.
- I have not confirmed from readable local PDF text that the paper states "mu=28 is better" as a universal setting.
- If the paper/table shows mu around 28, treat it as the author's case-specific bridge permeability or MEC result, not a general constant for this model.
- User's FEMM bridge samples for the current equivalent model are much lower: outer bridges about 14.3-14.6 and center bridge about 9.59, so blindly using mu=28 here would likely overestimate bridge permeability unless a new FEMM sample justifies it.

Current technical conclusion:
- Structure 2 dominates and is enough to get a useful Br waveform.
- Slotting geometry-only now creates correct tooth/slot ripple positions without deriving lambda from FEMM field.
- Remaining major error is Bt and sector-edge behavior.
- Structure 1 is not currently reliable; when added/fitted, it either needs nonphysical scaling or damages the middle region.
- Br-best gap p=1 now gives the best torque trend over radial-shift sweep; balanced gives better Bt but worse torque.
- Next high-value work: use Br-best analytical as physics baseline, then use PINN/inverse PINN or an explicit nonlinear bridge/leakage model to learn the remaining Bt/edge/saturation residual.

Future PINN direction:
- Treat the current Br-best no-load subdomain/semi-analytical model as the physics baseline.
- Use inverse PINN/PINN to learn residual terms for Bt, pole-edge behavior, bridge saturation/leakage, and later loaded armature-reaction effects.
- Do not replace the subdomain model with a black-box network; use PINN as a residual/surrogate layer on top of the analytical baseline.
